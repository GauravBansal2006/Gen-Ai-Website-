## Morning: boring consistency

Most progress is not cinematic. It is one solved problem, one page of notes, one small bug fixed before class.

My morning usually starts with DSA practice because it makes the rest of the day feel less foggy. Even thirty minutes changes the tone.

## Afternoon: lectures and experiments

College gives you random constraints: lab submissions, event meetings, attendance, surprise quizzes. The trick is to keep experiments small enough that they survive the schedule.

For AI learning, I keep tiny questions:

- Can I improve retrieval quality with better chunk titles?
- Can I make the UI explain uncertainty?
- Can I compare two prompts without fooling myself?

## Night: shipping energy

Late nights are for stitching things together. This is when the website gets polished, a README improves, or a prototype finally becomes demoable.

```md
Small daily proof beats vague ambition.
```

### The current rule

Build in public, but learn in private too. Not every rough note needs to become content immediately.
